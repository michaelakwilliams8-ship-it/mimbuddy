Australian Charter of Healthcare Rights

Every patient has the same rights

Under the Australian Charter of Healthcare Rights, every patient has the right to:

RightWhat it means for a neurodivergent patient
AccessCare that meets their needs, not removal when distress doesn't look calm
SafetyAn environment that feels safe, including the sensory environment
RespectTheir identity, communication style and choices respected
PartnershipDecisions made with them and the people they trust
InformationInformation they can understand, in a format that works for them
PrivacyTheir information kept private
Give feedbackA way to speak up without it affecting their care

Reasonable adjustments aren't optional. They're required under the Disability Discrimination Act 1992.

In New Zealand, the Code of Health and Disability Services Consumers' Rights includes the right to effective communication.

Why now

The NSQHS Standards are mandated in every Australian hospital, and they support the right of people with intellectual disability to equitable care. The third edition is in consultation, and disability health experts are calling for hospitals to be required to provide reasonable adjustments, train clinicians to provide them, and audit that they happen.

These rights aren't extra work. They're why you deserve the right tools.

The CARE Framework

  • C

    Communication

    Adapt to how each person communicates.

  • A

    Autonomy

    Presume competence and share decisions.

  • R

    Reduce barriers

    Predictability, fewer sensory demands.

  • E

    Empathy

    Take pain seriously and lead with curiosity.

What it does

Everything at the bedside

The MimBuddy brings the tools families and clinicians already trust into one standalone, joyful device. It was built with allied health therapists, nurses and neurodivergent families, replacing laminated cards, printed schedules, timers and apps.

Safe by design

A standalone hardware device, built that way for safety. No ads, no distractions, and therapeutic support out of the box.

  • Knows who I am

    About me
    A profile of who I am, how I communicate, what I need and what helps.
    Speaks my language
    A friendly MimBuddy mascot talks to me in the language that suits me best, like English or Chinese.
    Speaks to my needs
    Set up to use the language of my support needs: declarative language for a child with PDA, or safety and autonomy language for OCD and fear-based needs, like around food.

  • Shows me what's next

    Today's plan
    The day's flow, set up by staff. Today: X-ray.
    Visual routine boards
    Each step of a routine, one picture at a time.
    Social stories and procedures
    Customised stories and information about what's coming, like getting a blood test.
    Wait timers
    Waiting I can see, so I know how long is left.

  • Helps me say how I feel

    Feelings board
    Talk about and process my feelings, on my own or with the people caring for me.
    Pain scale board
    Show how much it hurts, in a way that makes sense to me.
    Pain visuals
    Show what my pain is and where it is.

  • Helps me feel safe and steady

    Calming activities
    Activities that help me up-regulate or down-regulate, depending on what I need.
    Daily living
    Support with everyday activities during admissions and ED visits.
    Staff profiles
    Get to know the people caring for me before I meet them.

How your hospital can improve today

  1. Ask at triage: “How do you communicate best? What helps when you're overwhelmed?”Mimbalu helps with this
  2. Treat parents and carers as experts. They know what calms their child.
  3. Reduce sensory load: a quieter space, dimmer lights, fewer people at once.
  4. Explain before you act. Predictability prevents distress.Mimbalu helps with this
  5. Offer other ways to communicate: AAC, a body map, a visual pain scale.Mimbalu helps with this
  6. Respond to distress with regulation, not security. Distress is communication.Mimbalu helps with this
  7. Train your team in CARE, with autistic-led organisations.
  8. Appoint a disability liaison officer families can turn to.

Mimbalu helps with 1, 4, 5 and 6 from the first shift.
